## What the reconstruction has been checked on

The whole of it is on this build: the 74 scenes load, the start, the language select, the movies, the new game, the
tutorial and chapter 1 run headless (`tests/vis/playthrough.cpp`) and in the SDL2 player. The cursor, the action text and
the dialog fonts of the SDL2 player were compared with a recorded playthrough of the original by the person who
watched it (no automatic comparison exists).

## Differences to other builds

- Deponia 2 Linux: [`../deponia2/VERSIONS.md`](../deponia2/VERSIONS.md).
- Windows and macOS builds of Deponia 1: not looked at. The things to compare first are the graphics layer (Direct3D or
  OpenGL, `Device` in `config.ini`), the sound (OpenAL, XAudio), the movie player (ffmpeg or Media Foundation) and the

### The loading screen (`FillLoadingScreen`, `TLoadingControl::Init`, `ShowLoadingScreen`; 18 Nov 2026)
Reconstructed from the listing (the three games read the same): `SLoadingScreen` is two `TSprite`s (the picture of the screen, `kLoadingSprite`, +0x00; the status bar, `kLoadingStatusSprite`, +0x50), whether the bar fills (`!kLoadingStatusCover`, +0xA0), the sound (`kLoadingMusic`, +0xA8), the place of the bar (+0xB0) and the window's size (+0xB8/+0xBC, put there by `ShowLoadingScreen`). `Init` loads the two pictures, puts the screen's in the middle of the window, starts the sound (volume 100, looping, music), puts the bar in its place and sets the part of it that is drawn (all of it; none for a bar that fills). The three functions were empty before: the loading screen drew nothing. Checked only that the picture is loaded (`sys_loadingscreen.webp` in the rabbit); the frames of the engine's own screen are not swapped before the game's first frame in the stand-alone player.

### The lists of the console (`PrintScenes`, `PrintCharacters`, `PrintConditions`, `PrintValues`; `PrintSounds` of the rabbit)
`vstables/recordPrints.cpp`, read in the listing (the four functions read the same in all three games: the tables 4, 0, 10 and 20, the fields 0x252/0x124/0x13B/0x13C/0x84, 0x1F6/0x253/0x1F7, 0xC2 and 0x148): each makes the lines of its table, sorted by name (`cmpName`: `TCharHolder::Cmp` < 0): "All Scenes:", for each `Scene: "name" (Id: n), Menu|Playable Scene, Brightness: n%`, the music line and an empty one; the characters (`Active: T|F`) and conditions (`Value: T|F`) and values likewise. An empty table says "This Game Has No ..." (the characters say "Scenes": the original's own message). `tests/vis/printtest.cpp` prints them for the real data. The rabbit's `TSoundBase::PrintSounds` has no fade text in its streaming line (`Streamed: T`). `tools/asm_strings.py` lists the strings a function refers to (wide ones too).

### `~TManagedObject` (25 Nov 2026; found by loading a savegame of the first room)
Loading the second savegame (and bookmark) of the first room crashed the stand-alone player: `TGAnimation::ClearAnimations` (called by `TGameControl::Load` through `LoadAnimations`) deleted a running animation, whose destructor tells its owners (`NotifyOwnersAnimationFinished`), and one of them was an object that no longer existed. The destructor of `TManagedObject` was `= default`; the original's (asm 192299-192394) lets the object go of everything that refers to it: `HideAnimation(_currentAnimation, this)`, `TGObjectManager::NotifyObjectRemoved(_objRef)`, `DetachOwner(this)` on each secondary animation and `TGText::DetachOwner` for its text. Now reconstructed that way (`TManagedObject.cpp`); Deponia 4 has the same destructor (asm 417700), the Rabbit's also hides each secondary animation after detaching (rabbit.asm 279496), so all three trees have it. Found with `tests/vis/slotload.cpp` (`slotload <b|s> <slot>`, run from the game folder with `GAME_PASSW`; it loads one slot of the real data headless; the savegames must be in `userdata/VisionaireStudio/Savegames`, the stub's directory, not the player's `userdata/Daedalic Entertainment/Deponia/Savegames`). Checked: the four slots of the first room's savegames crash at slot 1 (both kinds) before and load after; `loadtest` and the Rabbit's `plugintest` still pass. Not checked in the SDL player itself.

### The direction on each leg of a way (`TGWaySystem::GetNextPosition`; 25 Nov 2026)
Reported while play-testing: Rufus, loaded in the lower part of his home and sent to the top right, walked up the ramp (and its steeper part) still facing down-right. `GetNextPosition` works out the direction of a leg of the way when the leg starts, but it did so only when its "known" flag (+0x64) was clear. The flag is set when a step does not reach its target and is cleared only when the way goes on to the destination, so a way along several points kept the direction of its first leg. In the original (asm 249540-249794; `-O0` Deponia 4: `if (reachedTarget || !known)`) the direction is also worked out whenever the character stands exactly on the point it was heading for (the path 586618 -> 58664F -> 586670 calls `GetAngle` without looking at the flag). Now the same. Checked with `tests/vis/slotload.cpp` (`slotload s 1 1850 1350`: loads the savegame and walks the current character to a scene point, printing the direction every 10 steps): before, 336 on every step up to a later point; now 336, 36, 3, 138, 142, 60, 13, 344 as the legs change (up-right on the ramp, up-left on the steeper part). `tests/vis/waylegtest.cpp` checks the angles 0, 90, 180 of a way of three legs (3 failures without the fix). Applied to the Deponia 4 and Rabbit trees too (their originals have the same logic).

### The animation of a character's outfit (`TGAction::ShowAnimation`; 26 Nov 2026)
Reported while play-testing: combining the mousetrap and the provisions closes the inventory, but Rufus stands still instead of playing `combine_items_dv_r` (an animation of the outfit `Rufus_standard`, shared by the combining actions of the whole game). The action ran (the animation was started, its frame 26 started its action, the inventory came back), but on the wrong character: `ShowAnimation` looked the character up with `GetParent()` three steps above the animation (animation, outfit, character, *its* parent), the original two (asm 560610: `GetParent` twice from the animation). The extra step found no character and `GetCharacter` fell back to another one (`1008_Hannek` in this savegame), which played the animation out of sight. Now `GetCharacter(outfit.GetParent())`; `HideAnimation` already had it right. Found by comparing `rufus` (`GetCurrentCharacter`) with the `this` of `StartCharacterAnim` (gdb), and checked in the SDL player: the region of Rufus changes by 20-24 units between frames 220 and 410 where it did by 1-3, and the frames show him turned and working his hands over the items. Deponia 4 and the Rabbit have the same lookup in their originals (two `GetParent` calls before `GetCharacter`) and the same fix.

Debug aids added: `DEPONIA_ACTIONS=<frame>:<id of an action (table 7)>;...` in the Deponia 1 SDL player starts the action at that frame (and turns the traces on); `-s 0` loads `savegame00.dat` of `./userdata/Daedalic Entertainment/Deponia/Savegames` (relative to the current directory, so a scratch directory with a copy of a savegame as slot 0 does not touch the real ones); `tests/vis/slotload.cpp` takes `act <id>` (runs an action headless and prints what the current character plays), `tests/vis/namefind.cpp <text>...` lists the objects of the tables whose name has the text (items are table 6, actions table 7, animations table 9).

### Walking to a door that Wenzel stands in front of (Rathaus, 27 Nov 2026; no change of the engine)
Reported: in the latest savegame (the town hall, after the cutscene `ch01-04-i08 Wenzel kommt aus dem Bürgermeisterbüro`) a single click on the mayor's door makes Rufus walk up to it and stop; a double click enters. Checked with `slotload s 12 click e1014` (hover for 0.6 s, then click, then the game runs for 12 s):
- the saved game has `1011_wenzel_im_weg` = 1 and `1011_Rufus_aufgerufen` = 1 (both set at the end of that cutscene, which also makes `wegsystem_wenzel` the scene's way system) and `1011_Wenzel` standing at 997,662, in front of the door (the door object's position is 1005,615);
- the outline of `wegsystem_wenzel` has a large pocket (x 817-1083, y 598-830) that excludes the place in front of the door, so the door's position is outside it. `SetFreeDestination` goes to the nearest way point (`Point1`, 966,594, at the foot of the door), `CalculateWay` cannot see the door's position from it (`_walkingToWayPoint` stays on), the walk ends there, `ObjectReached` runs the door's event, `IsReached` (the character's position equals the object's: asm `TManagedObject::IsReached`, `TGObject::GetPosition`) is false, and `ClickedWithoutReach` has nowhere further to send Rufus. Each of these steps was compared with the original's listing (`THCharacter::OnEvent` for the destination object, `SetFreeDestination`, `CalculateWay`, `GetNextPosition`, `StopWalking`, `ObjectReached`, `GetActionsToTest`/`ExecuteMatchingAction`) and agrees with it;
- with `wegsystem_tueren_zu` or `wegsystem_normal` made the scene's (`way:303` / `way:127` in the test) the same click walks Rufus to 1005,615 (or the door action's own 979,596), the door action `'com_walk' angewandt` runs and he is in `1014 - Buero des Buergermeisters` after about 2 s. So the engine's walk-then-act path works; only the way system of the saved state stops it;
- the data sets `1011_wenzel_im_weg` back to 0 only in the dialog actions `D101_e03-c01` and `D101_e02-c05_lotek_weg` of `1014_Buergermeister`, and nothing else moves `1011_Wenzel` (`actiondump link:82`).
Not settled: whether the original lets a single click through while Wenzel stands there (the double click's action `Doppelklick (sofort)` does not look at the way system, which is why it works). Tools added: `slotload ... click <name or @x,y> [double] [way:<id>]`, `slotload ... cond <names>`, `actiondump link:<id>[:<command>]`.

### WORKAROUND: the mayor's door with Wenzel in front of it (28 Nov 2026)
The stop at the door described above is a known bug of the original game (the players' answer: do not click to go in, double-click; it is "the only spot in the game" that needs it). `TGObjectManager::ObjectReached(TGCharacter &, TVisObjRef &)` now takes a walk that ends at the way point next to that door as having reached it (`TManagedObject::SetReachBypass(true)` around the one `ObjectReached(object)`), so the action of the door runs. The conditions are all of: the name of the game is `Deponia`, the object is `e1014-|27|`, its scene `1011 - Rathaus`, the scene's way system `wegsystem_wenzel`, and the character is within 80 steps (the way point is 44 away) of the door's position. Nothing else changes: other objects, the other way systems of the scene (the door already worked with `wegsystem_tueren_zu`) and the other trees (Deponia 4, the Rabbit) are as before. Checked with `slotload s 12 click e1014`: in the saved state a single click now ends in `1014 - Buero des Buergermeisters` like the double click (before: stopped at 966,594); `way:303` and `double` unchanged; `waytest` and `waylegtest` pass. To be remembered for the ScummVM module: the check is on the game's own data, and the workaround can go with the Deponia 1 detection there.

### Savegames of the Windows build and of the stand-alone player compared (28 Nov 2026)
Two savegames from the start of the game (after the tutorial), one made by the GOG Windows build (engine build date Apr 30 2019, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Daedalic Entertainment\Deponia\Savegames\savegame00.dat`, 734,215 bytes) and one by the stand-alone player (733,412 bytes), were compared with `tests/vis/savedump.cpp` (loads a savegame over `data.vis`, prints the number of records of each table and the global script variables, writes all game data as XML, and with a third argument saves the loaded game again with our writer).
- **Container:** both are `VIS3` containers with the same password `SAVEGAMEPWD30` and two entries: the `VBIN` data and the WebP screenshot. The schema tree at the head of the data (282 nodes, revision 308, version 186) is identical; only the record counts differ (10,826 and 10,795).
- **Our writer is faithful:** loading the Windows savegame and saving it again with `TVisionaire::SaveSaveGame` gives **the same 721,463 bytes** of data as in the Windows file; the same for our own savegame (718,430 bytes). So the format, the order of the tables and of the records, and the fields written are as the original's for this data.
- **What differs** (all state, none the format): 15 records of the game data (the platform conditions `sys_platform_win`/`linux`/`isTouch` as the player's platform; `sys_doors_main_menu`, `sys_block_main_menu_mouseover`; the volumes `volume_sound`/`music`/`speech`/`movie`, `sys_ResolutionMode`; three random values); the global Lua variables (`sys_dirPath` and the path variable, `lfs` here and `curls`, `handles_count`, `loadlist`, `loadcount`, `max_loadcount`, `sys_salesChannel` there: the banner/curl script and the Galaxy sales channel run only on Windows; the volumes and the check time); the order of the Lua variables (the records of table 34 are ordered as Lua's table iteration gives them: 1,636 against 1,614); and the saved running state (table 24, the active texts: 19 against 10, the Windows build keeps the nine texts of the main menu's buttons that were shown there; table 26, 72 running animations in both, with the same names). Nothing of scenes, objects, characters, items, actions or conditions of the story differs.
- **Not explained:** why the Windows build shows no foreground objects after loading a savegame of ours. Nothing in the file separates it from a Windows one except the state listed above. The candidates to test, one class at a time in the Windows build with a copy of its own savegame changed that way: the platform conditions, the script variables (the paths and `lfs`), and the active texts.

### FOUND: the defaults of `TTObject::OnInit` (and three more) were 0, the original's are 1 / -1 / 100 (28 Nov 2026)
Why a savegame of the stand-alone player showed no objects when loaded in the Windows build: the content of the two fresh savegames as XML (`savedump <savegame> <out> <resaved> <savegame xml>`) differs in the fields that only a savegame has. In every one of the 4,314 objects `ObjectScale`, `ObjectScaleX` and `ObjectScaleY` are `0.000000` in ours and `1.000000` in the Windows build's, and 64 running animations have `AnimationSize` `0.000000` against `-1.000000`; an engine that takes the scale of an object as it is draws it at no size (our players read 0 as "no scale", which hid it). The cause was in `vstables/recordCallbacks.cpp`: `TTObject::OnInit` set the three scales to 0.0 where the original loads `dword_D33D44` = `3F800000h` = 1.0 (asm 1467490), and the same mistake in `TTButton::OnInit` (`kButtonScale`, `kButtonScaleX`, `kButtonScaleY`: 1.0), `TSAnimation::OnCreate` (`kAnimationSize`: -1.0) and `TTCharacter::OnInit` (`kCharacterSize`: 100.0). All the float defaults of the `OnInit`/`OnCreate` functions were compared with the originals of the three games (Deponia 1, Deponia 4, the Rabbit: they agree with each other) and the rest was right. Fixed in the three trees; a fresh game now writes 4,314 objects with scale 1.0.
What was ruled out first: the container and the schema are the same, our writer saves the Windows savegame again byte for byte, and one class at a time of the differences that are legitimate (the platform conditions, the volumes and settings, the global script variables) did not reproduce it in the Windows build. What stays different and is not a fault: the platform flags, the paths and volumes, the Windows-only banner/curl globals, the order of the Lua variables, the running texts, `CharacterFollowReachDistance` (the Windows build saves uninitialized memory there, ours 0). Savegames made before this fix keep the 0 scales; `savevariant ... fixscales` (tests/vis/savevariant.cpp, tools/savevariant.py) writes them as 1.0 and -1.0 again.

### WORKAROUND: Rufus shown in front in the tunnel of the Viaduct (28 Nov 2026)
Reported while play-testing, and seen in the Windows build too: clicking the right exit `e2007-|23|` of the Viaduct ("To the Outer Turn") has Rufus walk into the dark tunnel, appear in front of everything while he says that he tries to find his way by sound, and be hidden again when he walks out. Traced with `slotload s 37 click e2007` (`SKIPCLICKS=1 EVERYSTEP=1`: the draw list of the scene and the state of the tunnel objects at every step):
- the exit's action `'com_walk' angewandt` (id 27204) walks Rufus with the command "character goes to object" to `pos_rufus_nach_2007` (1361,672), says the texts, and walks him to `pos_rufus_von_2007` (1073,686);
- 1361,672 is inside the action area `steg` (id 148) of the scene (the places 1073,686 and 1176,686 are not); the walk enters it, the action of that area on entering sets the condition `2004_Rufus_auf_Steg`, and the one on leaving sets it back (ids 15351 and 15385);
- the object `bg_Aufgang_zum_Licht_dahinter` (the layer that hides Rufus in the tunnel, depth 704; Rufus's is his y, 672) has that condition negated and is not shown while it is true: it went inactive at the step Rufus reached the place and active again when he left it. Rufus's own place in the draw list (index 28 of 82, before the layer) did not change; the layer simply is not there while he speaks.
The depth sort (`TGScene::SortAllObjects`, asm 171805) and the area code (`UpdateActionAreas`) are the original's, so this is the original's data and engine behaving as the game's authors did not mean. The fix (`isScriptedWalkIntoTheTunnel` in `TGCharacter.cpp`, called by `TGCharacter::UpdateActionAreas`): the entering of the area `steg` is not acted on (the area is entered, its action does not run) when all of: the name of the game is `Deponia`, the area is `steg`, the character is `Rufus` in the scene `2004 - Viaduct`, and the action `'com_walk' angewandt` of the object `e2007-|23|` is running (`TGAction::IsFinished()` was added for this). The leaving action still runs and only sets the condition back to what it is. Checked: during the exit's sequence the layer is active at every step (it was inactive for about 50); a walk into the area by hand (`click @1361,672`) still switches the layer off for as long as Rufus stands there; `loadtest`, `waytest` and `waylegtest` pass.
Test tool additions: `slotload ... click ALL` lists all objects of the scene with depth, position, condition (`*` is expanded by the Windows C runtime), `SHOWAREAS=1` lists the action areas and which of them hold given places, `SKIPCLICKS` and `EVERYSTEP` (see above).
